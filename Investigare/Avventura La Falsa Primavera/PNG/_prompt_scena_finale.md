# Prompt scena finale — Shūichi in cella (alba 01/04/1997)

Immagine evocativa, **non grafica**. La scena rappresenta la **scoperta** del suicidio di Shūichi la mattina dopo l'arresto di Yumi. Il corpo non è visibile. Il racconto passa per i dettagli: la cella vuota all'alba, gli oggetti personali piegati con cura, la luce pallida che entra dalla finestra alta. Il giapponese che si toglie la vita lascia le cose in ordine — è quel silenzio compositivo che racconta l'evento senza mostrarlo.

## Specifiche tecniche

- 35mm film photograph, **Kodak Tri-X 400 black and white** (pushed one stop), **OR** Fujicolor Superia 400 desaturated
- Camera: Nikon F3 / 35mm f/2 prime lens (wider than portraits — environment-driven)
- Cold pre-dawn light from a single high small window, no artificial light, blue-grey palette if color
- Visible film grain, slight underexposure, deep shadows
- Documentary realism, no stylization, **NO body, NO violence, NO graphic content**
- Quiet, contemplative, somber

## Negative prompt

> any human body, corpse, blood, violence, graphic content, dramatic lighting, cinematic composition, color grading, HDR, modern interior, contemporary cell design, decorative elements, sentimentality, posed objects, studio look.

## Prompt principale — vista interna cella vuota all'alba

> Empty Japanese police holding cell (留置場 ryūchijō) at first light, photographed inside Higashiyama Keisatsu-sho, Kyoto, April 1st 1997. Shot on 35mm Kodak Tri-X 400 black and white film with a Nikon F3 camera and 35mm f/2 prime lens, cold pre-dawn natural light entering through a small high barred window on the upper right wall, deep shadows, visible film grain, no artificial lighting, slight underexposure, somber documentary realism.
>
> SETTING: small rectangular detention cell, approximately 3×2 meters. Pale tatami mat floor showing wear marks. Plain unpainted concrete walls, a single small high rectangular window with vertical metal bars near the ceiling on the right side, casting a faint pale rectangle of dawn light onto the floor. The cell door, a heavy steel sliding mechanism with a small inspection slot, is partially open on the left edge of the frame, suggesting an officer has just arrived but is not visible.
>
> OBJECTS, COMPOSED WITH JAPANESE QUIET ORDER (the elements that tell the story without showing it):
> - A folded futon mattress and blanket placed neatly along the back wall, edges aligned with precision — the bedding has been used and then carefully restored before something happened
> - A pair of plain rubber slippers (provided by the facility) set side by side at the edge of the tatami, perfectly parallel
> - A small wooden tray on the floor near the door with an untouched morning meal: a covered bowl of rice, a small dish of pickled vegetables, a paper cup of tea with steam no longer rising, the wrapper of disposable chopsticks still sealed
> - A folded prison-issue cotton jacket placed on top of the futon, sleeves arranged inward
> - On the floor near the futon, a single sheet of paper folded in three, edges aligned (a brief written note left behind, content not legible from this angle)
> - No personal belongings beyond what the facility provided — wallets, belts, watches and shoelaces have been removed at intake (per protocol), and are absent from the cell
>
> ATMOSPHERE: pre-dawn silence, the kind that follows something irreversible. Cold air. The pale window light hits only one corner of the floor. The rest of the cell is in soft grey gradient. No human figure, no body, no violence, no blood — the photograph is about absence and order. The composition reads as documentary evidence intake, the dispassionate frame of a morning shift discovering what the night left behind.
>
> COMPOSITION: low-medium angle, eye-level with the futon, slight wide-angle distortion at the edges from the 35mm lens, the partially open cell door framing the left third, the meal tray in the foreground, the futon and folded jacket as the visual center, the small barred window as a faint distant secondary light source. Black and white tonal range deep but never theatrical — flat documentary truth.

## Prompt alternativo — vista esterna corridoio cella

> Long corridor of a Japanese police holding cell block (留置場), Higashiyama Keisatsu-sho, Kyoto, early morning April 1st 1997, 06:15. Shot on 35mm Kodak Tri-X 400 black and white film with a Nikon F3 camera and 35mm f/2 prime lens, cold fluorescent corridor light overhead mixed with pale dawn light from a small window at the far end of the hallway, visible film grain, somber documentary realism.
>
> SETTING: narrow institutional corridor with pale linoleum floor scuffed by years of use, plain pale green-grey painted walls, a row of identical heavy steel sliding cell doors on the right side, each with small inspection slots and metal numbering plates (seen in the foreground: cell **七** [seven], the next one **八** [eight] partially in frame). Plain wooden notice board on the left wall with worn paper notices in Japanese.
>
> FIGURE: a single uniformed Japanese police officer, mid-fifties, in standard 1997 KPD navy-blue uniform with peaked cap held in one hand, stands with his back to the camera in front of cell **七**, hand on the door handle, pausing before sliding it fully open. Posture frozen mid-motion — the kind of stillness of a man who has just understood what he is about to find. His shoulders are slightly tense. We never see his face.
>
> ATMOSPHERE: the institutional silence of a detention block at dawn. No other figures, no movement, no sound implied. The corridor stretches into soft grey haze toward the far window. The composition is observational — the camera is several meters behind the officer, neither following him nor confronting him, simply witnessing the moment the night's outcome becomes morning's report.
>
> COMPOSITION: deep one-point perspective along the corridor, the officer at the visual center but slightly off-axis, the cell door **七** as the focal point, foreground floor in soft shadow, dawn window at vanishing point as a faint pale rectangle. Black and white tonal range with deep shadows in the corners and middle grey throughout the corridor.

## Note d'uso

- **Quale scegliere**: il prompt principale (vista interna cella) è più immediato e racconta più storia con gli oggetti. L'alternativo (corridoio + agente di spalle) è più trattenuto, più cinematografico, e si presta meglio se l'immagine deve introdurre la chiusura della sessione senza imporre una lettura.
- **Versione colore**: se preferito, sostituire *Kodak Tri-X 400 black and white* con *Fujicolor Superia 400 desaturated, cool blue-grey palette, slight green cast from old fluorescent corridor light*.
- **Mai mostrare**: corpo, sangue, oggetti del suicidio (cintura, lenzuola annodate, etc.). La scena è già descritta in `Storia Completa.md` sez. 9 — *"Non descrivere il suicidio con dettagli grafici. Basta il colpo, il silenzio, e il vuoto dopo."* Il prompt rispetta questa regola.
- **Quando usarla in tavola**: solo se i PG hanno raggiunto **Esito A** della scena finale (Shūichi calmato → suicidio in cella). Mostrarla in silenzio, senza commento, dopo che Yamada o un collega comunica la notizia ai PG la mattina dopo.
